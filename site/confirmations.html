Confirmations and permissions

OpenLAMT does not lock settings away. Instead, every action that can hurt asks first, and the more it can hurt, the more it asks. This page explains the levels, and what accounts with limited permissions see.

The confirmation levels

LevelWhat you seeEnter confirms
noneThe action runs at once. The result appears in the status bar or a short message.–
simpleA red symbol, a sentence about the effect, Cancel and a red action button.No
questionA blue or orange symbol above the title and a blue main button. Used where you choose between two normal paths.Blue: yes. Orange: no
orangeAn orange warning box, often the change as “old → new”, and the checkbox I understand the risk for Lab-Server (192.0.2.10). The red button works only after you tick it.No
redA red warning box that starts with a bold sentence, extra fields where needed, and the same checkbox.No

The checkbox names the device with its address. That way you notice when the wrong window is in front. Cancel is always the safe choice, and Esc cancels every dialog.

Where a device requires user consent, the consent code comes on top of these levels: for remote sessions, Reboot to BIOS, Boot Options and Erase drives, OpenLAMT asks for the code after your confirmation and before it changes anything.

Which action asks what

LevelActions
nonePower On, screenshot, recording, network scan, export, Check Now, Update, reading in the WSMAN Browser, Sync Now, Respond to ping, User consent, turning a remote feature on, switching One Click Recovery or Remote Platform Erase, adding a wake alarm, importing a certificate, adding a forwarding target, the audit log settings (except below), a power package that keeps remote power-on, the idle timeout, Wi-Fi state and profiles (except below), adding System Defense filters, policies and watchdogs, activating a policy that drops nothing, turning a policy off
simplePower Off, Reset, Power Cycle, Reboot to BIOS (not when the device is off), Shutdown, Sleep, Hibernate, Boot Options (the dialog itself is the confirmation), deleting a wake alarm, a Wi-Fi profile, a System Defense policy, filter or watchdog, an unused certificate or key, or a forwarding target, deleting a favorite, quitting with open KVM sessions
questionBoot from ISO and Restart from ISO, turning on SOL or media redirection from the device window, closing a window with an ISO mounted, import, sending a password, a Wi-Fi key, a private key or a drive password without TLS (orange symbol)
orangeHost name, domain, Dynamic DNS, turning KVM, IDE-R or SOL off, the TLS certificate, editing your own account, deleting a user, a power package without remote power-on, activating a System Defense policy that drops traffic (also through a watchdog), deleting a trusted root certificate, HTTPS boot from a file on this computer, switching the audit log off
redThe admin account, the network address, environment detection, turning AMT off, deleting a Wi-Fi profile or turning Wi-Fi off while OpenLAMT reaches the device over Wi-Fi, clearing the audit log, erasing the drives

“Don’t show again”

Some messages offer Don’t show again, for example the warning before a password goes out without TLS. Confirmations with a checkbox never do. To get every hidden message back, click Reset Warnings in Settings.

Limited accounts

An AMT account may hold only some permissions, for example only KVM, media, console. OpenLAMT connects with such an account and shows what it may not read, instead of failing.

  • The device window says “Power: not allowed”, “AMT: not allowed” or “Control mode: not allowed” in the status bar. The favorite shows “Not allowed for this account” and a grey ring.
  • A management page the account may not read says “This account is not allowed to read this information on the device.”
  • On the Settings page, sections the account may not read show N/A. Their switches are off and disabled, their choices show no selection, and their buttons are disabled. Readable sections work as usual.
  • An action the account may not run ends with “This account is not allowed to do this on the device.”

Power actions, KVM, media and the console only need their own permission. A Redirection-only account can therefore use the remote screen, mount an ISO and open the console, even though it cannot read the settings.
