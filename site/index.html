OpenLAMT User Guide

OpenLAMT is a local desktop app for Intel AMT. It switches your devices on and off, shows their screen and serial console, boots them from an ISO and manages their AMT settings. It runs on macOS, Windows and Linux, without a browser and without a server.

What OpenLAMT does

  • Power and boot: power on, power off, reset, power cycle, shut down, sleep, hibernate, reboot into the BIOS, boot from an ISO, boot options for the next start (PXE, BIOS, HTTPS boot) and One Click Recovery from an image on your computer.
  • Remote screen (KVM): keyboard, video and mouse of the device, also in the BIOS. Take screenshots and record videos.
  • Serial console (SOL): the text console of the device, for example a Linux login prompt.
  • Media (IDE-R): mount an ISO file or a folder on the device as a CD drive and boot from it.
  • Device management: hardware, event log with event forwarding, audit log with its settings, AMT settings with an ⓘ help for each, erasing the drives, user accounts, power policy and wake alarms, Wi-Fi profiles, System Defense with the agent watchdog, certificates, and a browser for the raw AMT classes.
  • AI agents: openlamt-mcp lets an AI agent use your favorites, with the limits you set.
  • Favorites: keep your devices with their settings. Passwords stay in the system keychain.

OpenLAMT is a client for one person at one computer. It talks to each device directly on the network. It has no server, no cloud account and no remote access through the internet (no CIRA or MPS).

Which version this guide describesThe guide follows the newest development state of OpenLAMT. If your copy lacks a feature described here, update to the newest release.

How to read this guide

  • The question mark symbol at the bottom of the sidebar opens this guide in your browser, in the language of the app.
  • Names of buttons, menus and fields are in bold, exactly as the app shows them in English.
  • The screens are drawn in the look of the app. They follow the light or dark appearance of this page. On a small screen, swipe a screen sideways to see all of it.
  • The labels simple, question, orange and red name the confirmation an action asks for. Confirmations and permissions explains them.
  • All names and addresses are examples: example.com, 192.0.2.0/24, “ACME Lab”.
